# Ground brief — cross-border-remittance-cost

**Status:** grounded (2026-07-31)  
**Vertical:** fintech  
**Rank:** 7  
**Claim (particular):** Advertised fee cuts do not automatically mean cheap remittances. Total cost includes FX margin, receiving-side fees, float, and corridor structure. Transparency and competition on **total price** matter more than a single headline fee.

## Who is arguing what

| Actor | Position | Source |
|-------|----------|--------|
| CPSS (now CPMI) & World Bank (2007) | General Principles for International Remittance Services: transparency of total price; contestable markets lower cost | bis.org/cpmi/publ/d76.pdf |
| World Bank Remittance Prices Worldwide | Public measurement of corridor prices | remittanceprices.worldbank.org |
| SDG 10.c agenda (via WB remittance work) | Policy goal to reduce remittance costs | WB remittances topic framing |

## Facts that must appear

1. **Remittances defined as cross-border person-to-person, relatively low value** (CPSS/WB 2007).
2. **GP1 stress on transparency:** publicize speed and **total price**, including fees that may hit the receiver.
3. **Correspondent/open services** can leave the sender without knowledge of receiving-side fees or total time (CPSS/WB analysis).
4. **Competition / contestable markets** framed as the main path to lower prices (CPSS/WB).
5. **World Bank RPW** exists as the price-transparency measurement system operators and policymakers use.

## Theory hook

Information asymmetry on FX and all-in price; corridor economics (two-sided density); platform/MTO control of last-mile cash-out.

## Real case

No invented MTO. Use the CPSS/WB description of open vs closed services and RPW as the public scoreboard.
