## Facts that must appear

1. **CFPB defines BNPL for the 2022 report as installment products typically repaid in four or fewer payments (pay-in-four framing in later work).**  
   Supports: 2022 report + 2025 study framing.

2. **2021: 10.5% of unique users charged at least one late fee (up from 7.8% in 2020); approval rates 73% in 2021.**  
   Supports: CFPB 15 Sep 2022 newsroom study summary.

3. **2022: 21.2% of consumers with a credit record used BNPL; 63% originated multiple simultaneous loans; 33% used multiple lenders; ~two-thirds of loans to subprime/deep subprime applicants.**  
   Supports: CFPB Jan 2025 research release / report.

4. **Charge-off rates for named issuer cohorts rose from ~1.4% FY’20 toward ~3% by H1/FY’22 in CFPB’s 2022 report examples.**  
   Supports: CFPB 2022 PDF (dollar charge-off narrative).

5. **BNPL often not fully visible on traditional credit files — stacking risk hard for outside underwriters.**  
   Supports: CFPB 2025 study motivation (reporting gap).

## Theory hook

- **Consumer credit cycles:** vintage and unemployment sensitivity, not take-rate optimization.
- **Behavioral pricing / present bias:** installment framing reduces perceived debt.
- **Information asymmetry:** non-reporting of BNPL to CRAs enables multi-lender stacking.

Use CFPB market orders data and matched credit-file study — not invented lender anecdotes. Attribute issuer charge-off paths as CFPB-reported examples, not named speculation beyond the report’s language.
