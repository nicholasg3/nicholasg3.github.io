# Ground brief — open-banking-data-portability

**Status:** grounded (2026-07-31)  
**Vertical:** fintech  
**Claim (particular):** Open banking / personal financial data rights rules reallocate distribution power: the incumbent holds the account of record, but forced portability lets authorized third parties compete for the relationship — so “who owns the customer” becomes a fight over authorization, liability, and interface control, not branding.

## Facts that must appear in the essay (with support)

1. **CFPB finalized Personal Financial Data Rights rule on 22 October 2024** implementing CFPA section 1033.  
2. **Rule requires banks and other data providers to unlock covered data for consumers and authorized third parties** in electronic form, with third-party obligations on collection, use, and retention.  
3. **Covered products in the rule’s public framing include bank accounts, credit cards, mobile wallets, payment apps** (per CFPB press).  
4. **Customer “ownership” splits:** account of record and complaint duty often stay with the data provider; UX and advice can move to the authorized third party.  
5. **Reconsideration / litigation / political risk** means portability timelines are not a fixed engineering calendar.

## Theory hook

- Forced interoperability / data portability as multi-sided platform regulation.
- Principal–agent: consumer authorizes a third party; bank remains regulated data provider.
