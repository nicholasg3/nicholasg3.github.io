# Ground brief — stablecoin-privacy-traceability

**Status:** grounded (2026-07-31)  
**Vertical:** fintech  
**Rank:** 2  
**Claim (particular):** Public-chain retail stablecoin transfers create transaction graphs that counterparties, analytics firms, and authorities can read more easily than cash. That transparency is a compliance feature for some institutions and a privacy cost for others.

## Who is arguing what

| Actor | Position | Source |
|-------|----------|--------|

| Privacy advocates / cash comparison (discourse) | Cash is peer-to-peer and less graphable; public ledgers invert that | (argument structure; not a single primary) |

## Facts that must appear

1. **Stablecoins are a core rail in crypto intermediation** (BIS DeFi article).
2. **Public ledgers make transaction graphs analyzable at scale** — Chainalysis states these crime estimates are not possible the same way in traditional finance.
3. **Issuers retain freeze/blacklist and disclosure roles** — centralization is not only a bug for privacy debates; it is how compliance is often delivered.
4. **Use-case split:** AML/investigators and institutional risk teams treat graph visibility as a feature; retail users seeking cash-like privacy experience it as a cost.

## Theory hook

Payment **transparency vs financial privacy**; public-good properties of cash vs account-based digital money; “surveillance is a property of the rail,” not only of the app UI.

## Real cases

- **Chainalysis annual crime measurement** as existence proof of industrial-scale chain analytics on public ledgers.
- **BIS DeFi “decentralisation illusion”** as institutional framing that governance and concentration matter for policy.
