Oracle failure is not automatically an insured event

Marketing &quot;insured smart contracts&quot; fails when policy wording excludes the actual failure mode. Map scenarios to triggers before you sell comfort.

The claim

Smart-contract and oracle failures create loss events that look technical and feel financial. Insurance language often does not map cleanly onto those failure modes.

If your DeFi or tokenized product assumes someone else pays when the oracle breaks, read the exclusions.

How to ground it

Traditional cyber and crime policies may exclude blockchain consensus failures, economic design bugs, or oracle manipulation depending on wording.

Specialized cover exists in niches; capacity, claims history, and basis risk remain hard.

Operator consequences

Risk transfer is not risk removal. Controls on oracle design, circuit breakers, and pause authority still matter more than a PDF certificate.

What to do

Map loss scenarios to policy triggers with counsel and brokers before you market "insured."

Prefer engineering mitigation for high-frequency failure modes; use insurance for residual tail risk if available.

Source notes

  • IAIS: International insurance supervisory association hub.
  • NIST Cybersecurity Framework: Control vocabulary useful when underwriting tech failure.
  • SEC: When products touch securities/markets, disclosure of tech risk matters.
