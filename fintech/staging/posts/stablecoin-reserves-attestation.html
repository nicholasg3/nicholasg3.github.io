The stablecoin product is the reserve regime

Issuing a token is easy. The product is what backs it, who can redeem, how often someone attests, and what happens in a run. Treat attestation quality as the core feature, not a compliance afterthought.

The claim

Most stablecoin pitch decks lead with chain, yield, and integrations. Those matter only after users believe the liability is money-like under stress.

Reserve composition, segregation, redemption rights, and attestation cadence are the product features that survive a risk-off week. Everything else is distribution.

What &quot;attestation&quot; is not

An attestation is not a full audit of all risks. It is a time-stamped statement about reserve holdings under a defined scope. Scope, independence, lag, and whether it covers liabilities as well as assets all change the information value.

Marketing language that says "fully backed" without linking to the actual report and the legal entity that redeems is incomplete product disclosure.

Run dynamics still apply

Money-like liabilities face first-mover incentives when confidence drops. If redemption is gated, delayed, or only available to large counterparties, retail holders discover they held a different product than the homepage implied.

Compare not only asset mix (cash, T-bills, other) but who has contractual claim priority and in which jurisdiction.

What to do

If you issue or integrate a stablecoin, maintain a living memo: reserve assets, custodian, attestation firm, report lag, redemption eligibility, and insolvency treatment hypotheses.

If you cannot fill those fields from primary documents, you do not understand the product well enough to put customer funds on it.
