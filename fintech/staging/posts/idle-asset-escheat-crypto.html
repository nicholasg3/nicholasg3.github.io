Unclaimed-property law meets exchange custody

State escheat and unclaimed-property regimes were built for bank accounts and safe-deposit boxes. Exchange-held crypto inherits those rules awkwardly — and the risk shows up as a balance-sheet and customer-service problem, not a whitepaper problem.

The claim

Digital-asset platforms hold customer property under terms of service and local licensing. Separately, US states (and other jurisdictions) run unclaimed-property programs that can require reporting and remittance of idle assets after dormancy periods.

When those regimes map onto wallets and exchange accounts, operators face operational questions: what counts as customer contact, how do you transfer crypto to a state, and who bears price moves during dormancy disputes?

Why this is product risk

Customers experience escheat as "the exchange took my coins." States experience non-compliance as a reporting failure. Legal teams experience a mismatch between private-key custody and paper-era property statutes.

This is not theoretical for large platforms. It is a recurring diligence topic in public filings and state enforcement patterns around digital assets and unclaimed property.

What good operators do

Define dormancy clocks, contact attempts, and asset conversion policies in plain language. Log outreach. Know which entity is the holder of record in each state.

Do not invent a novel "crypto escheat protocol" in marketing. Align with counsel on actual state holder duties.

What to do

If you custody user assets, maintain a state-by-state unclaimed-property matrix next to your AML matrix. Same seriousness, different statute family.

If you are a user evaluating platforms, read the user agreement sections on abandoned accounts before you treat long-idle balances as risk-free storage.

Source notes

  • National Association of Unclaimed Property Administrators (NAUPA): Industry/government association hub for US unclaimed-property programs.
  • IRS (tax context only): Tax treatment is separate from escheat; do not conflate. Use state holder guides for escheat duties.
  • SEC EDGAR: Search large exchange/issuer filings for unclaimed property / escheat risk factor language as secondary evidence of industry practice.
  • NYDFS: Example state financial regulator context when the custodian is a supervised entity.
