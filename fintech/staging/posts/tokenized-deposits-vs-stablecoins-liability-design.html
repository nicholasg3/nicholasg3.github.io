Tokenized deposits and stablecoins are different liabilities

Both can move on shared ledgers. One is typically a bank deposit claim. The other is usually an issuer liability backed by reserves under a non-bank perimeter. Confusing the UX for the balance sheet is how bad products get built.

The claim

Tokenization can put different claims on similar rails. The legal claim is the product: who owes you, under what insolvency regime, and how redemption works.

Calling everything "on-chain dollars" erases the distinction supervisors care about.

Deposit vs reserve-backed token

A tokenized deposit remains a bank deposit if the structure preserves that claim — with deposit insurance rules and bank resolution regimes applying as designed.

A fiat stablecoin is usually a claim on an issuer or trust structure holding reserves. Run dynamics and supervisory tools differ.

Why builders blur them

Shared wallets and 24/7 transfer UX make the instruments feel identical to users. That is exactly when disclosure and naming matter.

Treasury and risk teams should not share models across instruments without adjusting for claim type.

What to do

In architecture docs, label instruments by liability type first, chain second.

In customer UX, do not imply deposit insurance on non-deposits. That error is both product and regulatory risk.
