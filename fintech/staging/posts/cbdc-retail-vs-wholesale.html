Retail CBDC and wholesale CBDC solve different problems

Retail CBDC debates privacy and public money access. Wholesale CBDC debates settlement finality among institutions. Collapsing them into one &quot;digital currency&quot; story confuses product design and politics.

The claim

Central bank digital currency is not one product. Retail CBDC is a claim ordinary people might hold on the central bank or through intermediaries. Wholesale CBDC is a settlement asset for banks and other eligible institutions.

Those designs share cryptography and ledgers in marketing decks. They do not share the same political economy, privacy tradeoffs, or balance-sheet consequences.

What BIS materials actually separate

BIS work on CBDCs consistently distinguishes retail and wholesale use cases, and often emphasizes the two-tier model where private intermediaries still face the public.

When a founder or investor says "CBDC will replace banks/stablecoins/cards," ask which CBDC. Wholesale settlement improvements do not automatically imply retail wallet competition.

Why the distinction matters for builders

Retail designs raise questions about identity, offline payments, holding limits, and disintermediation risk for deposits. Wholesale designs raise questions about atomic settlement, cross-border corridors, and who sits in the RTGS club.

Stablecoin and tokenized-deposit products compete more directly with pieces of these designs than with "money" as an abstraction. Map the liability, not the logo.

What to do

In product strategy docs, never write "CBDC risk" as a single line. Split: retail public money, wholesale settlement, and private tokenized liabilities.

Read central-bank pilots for the design choices (intermediated vs direct, holding caps, programmability limits), not for launch date theater.
