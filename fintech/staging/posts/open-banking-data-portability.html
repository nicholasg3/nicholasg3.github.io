Open banking rewrites who owns the customer relationship

Forced account-data portability is not a neutral API story. It reallocates distribution power between banks, fintechs, and aggregators — and turns &quot;who owns the customer&quot; into a contract and liability fight.

The claim

Open banking and financial data-rights rules force account-holding institutions to share customer-permissioned data with third parties. That changes who can underwrite, pitch, and switch products without owning the deposit account.

The technical story is APIs. The business story is distribution rent and complaint ownership when data is wrong or access is abused.

Jurisdiction is the product boundary

UK Open Banking, EU PSD2/PSR trajectories, and US personal financial data rights rules are related but not interchangeable. Consent, liability, screen-scraping phase-outs, and fee debates differ.

A "global open banking strategy" that ignores those differences is a branding exercise. Build country-by-country permission and liability maps.

Who owns the complaint

When a fintech uses bank-sourced data to open credit or move money, customers still experience a single brand. Supervisors and courts care which legal entity failed which duty.

Contracts that only cover API uptime and ignore error handling, re-auth, and fraud dispute paths create silent product risk.

What to do

For every open-banking feature, write: data categories used, legal basis, retention, who faces the customer on errors, and what happens when consent is revoked mid-flow.

Treat bank partners as infrastructure suppliers with political constraints, not as passive pipes.
