Cards and A2A rails price different liability models

A checkout button can hide whether you are on card messaging with dispute rights or account-to-account finality. Those are different products.

The claim

Cards and account-to-account rails are not interchangeable pipes. Messaging, dispute rights, economics, and authentication differ.

Product teams that treat "pay buttons" as one abstraction rediscover those differences in fraud and chargeback season.

How to ground it

Network rulebooks and realtime payment operator docs describe authorization, clearing, settlement, and exception handling differently.

FedNow and other A2A systems optimize for speed and finality; cards embed consumer dispute paths that merchants price as risk.

Operator consequences

Moving volume from cards to A2A can cut fees and increase irrevocable scam loss. The unit economics must include both.

What to do

Choose rails by liability model and user segment, not only by basis points.

Instrument success rate, fraud rate, and dispute rate per rail separately.

Source notes

  • FedNow: US A2A realtime service primary.
  • Federal Reserve Payment Systems: Broader US payments context.
  • BIS CPMI: Cross-country payment system comparisons.
  • EMVCo: Card payment technical standards hub.
