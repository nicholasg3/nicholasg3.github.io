The Travel Rule is market structure, not paperwork

FATF Travel Rule obligations for virtual asset service providers change who can transact with whom at scale. Compliance interoperability becomes a competitive moat and a fragmentation risk.

The claim

Once VASPs must collect and transmit originator and beneficiary information above thresholds, "open crypto rails" become gated by compliance counterparties.

That is market structure: it shapes liquidity venues, cross-border corridors, and which startups can touch institutional flow.

What FATF materials define

FATF recommendations and virtual-asset guidance set the international standard many national rules implement. National law still decides exact thresholds and enforcement.

Read the guidance for the obligation shape. Read your host regulator for the binding rule.

Competitive effects

Shared messaging protocols and vendor networks can reduce cost. They can also create oligopoly in compliance plumbing and exclude smaller VASPs from key corridors.

Founders should underwrite Travel Rule connectivity like they underwrite banking sponsors.

What to do

Map which counterparties you can legally send to today, what data you must attach, and what breaks if a vendor drops you.

Do not treat Travel Rule as a post-Series-B chore if you plan cross-border VASP volume.

Source notes

  • FATF — Updated Guidance for a Risk-Based Approach to Virtual Assets and VASPs (2021): Core guidance text for Travel Rule expectations; confirm latest edition on fatf-gafi.org.
  • MAS digital assets materials (example national implementation context): Illustrative national perimeter; confirm current MAS pages for binding rules.
  • BIS — Stablecoins risks/regulation: Adjacent institutional frame for crypto-asset regulatory perimeter.
