Bank–fintech deals sell distribution, not just sponsorship

Banking-as-a-service and sponsor-bank models are often pitched as API access. The scarce asset is usually the bank's charter, balance sheet, and regulatory relationships — rented distribution of public trust.

The claim

A fintech can look like a bank to users while a chartered institution holds deposits or issues cards. That split enables speed. It also creates dependency and supervisory concentration risk.

When many fintechs share a few sponsor banks, operational or enforcement problems become sector events.

What supervisors emphasize

Third-party risk management, BSA/AML ownership, and clarity on which entity is the bank. Guidance evolves; read current OCC/Fed/FDIC materials rather than 2019 blog posts.

If your growth plan assumes infinite sponsor capacity, you are underwriting a market that has already shown constraints.

Commercial reality

Banks charge for risk and compliance load, not only for core API calls. Fintechs that treat the bank as a dumb pipe get repriced or exited.

Diversifying sponsors is operationally hard and still wise past a certain scale.

What to do

In partnership memos, price regulatory rent explicitly: capital, compliance headcount, audit rights, and termination clauses.

Build a wind-down plan that protects customers if the sponsor relationship ends.

Source notes

  • OCC: Primary for national bank third-party and fintech partnership supervision themes.
  • Federal Reserve Supervision: Bank holding company / state member bank supervisory context.
  • FDIC: Deposit insurance and bank supervision context for partner banks.
  • Basel Committee — Principles for sound management of operational risk / third-party themes: International supervisory vocabulary for third-party risk.
