Remittance ads sell fees. The product is corridor control.

Lower advertised FX fees hide spread and float. The real unit economics sit in who controls the corridor, the last-mile cash-out, and the compliance fixed costs.

The claim

Retail remittance products compete on a headline fee. That number is usually the least informative part of the price.

What users actually pay is a bundle: explicit fee, FX spread, float timing, cash-out cost, and sometimes a partner markup at the receiving end. Who captures that bundle depends on corridor control, not app branding.

What the World Bank data is for

The World Bank Remittance Prices Worldwide database tracks average costs by corridor and instrument. It is the best public benchmark for comparing advertised and effective costs across corridors over time.

Use it to ground claims about "cheaper rails," not as a substitute for your own unit economics. Global averages hide corridor-specific monopoly and cash-out frictions.

Where the spread lives

In many corridors, the last mile still runs through agents, banks, or mobile-money operators with local licenses and physical cash. That is where distribution rent and FX inventory risk sit.

Digital-first senders often buy wholesale liquidity from those same rails. Compressing the consumer fee without owning cash-out just relocates margin to a partner you do not control.

What to do

When you evaluate a remittance product, map the full path: send-side KYC entity, FX book, settlement currency, receive-side license, cash-out agent, and who eats FX volatility for 24–72 hours.

If the pitch is only "we cut fees with crypto/rails X," ask who holds inventory and who owns the complaint when the payout fails. That answer is the product.

Source notes

  • World Bank — Remittance Prices Worldwide: Primary public dataset for corridor remittance cost comparisons.
  • World Bank — Migration and remittances data: Macro remittance volume context; pair with price database.
  • BIS CPMI — Cross-border retail payments: Institutional frame for frictions in cross-border retail payment chains.
  • FSB — Cross-border payments programme: G20 roadmap context for cost/speed/access/transparency targets.
