What Backs a Stablecoin When Its Bank Fails

Issuing a token is easy. The hard part is the reserve behind it, and markets reprice the liability when reserve access or redemption quality fails, even when the homepage says "fully backed".

By Nicholas Garcia — Lecturer, NUS School of Computing · Deputy Director, NUS FinTech Lab

NEW YORK — Stablecoin decks still open with the chain, the yield and the integrations. None of that counts until users and integrators trust the liability under stress. That trust rests on quieter questions: what sits in reserve, who holds it, who can redeem at par, how often an independent accountant attests, and what happens when a custodian bank fails over a weekend.

Supervisors have already set out those questions. On 8 June 2022 the New York State Department of Financial Services (DFS) published guidance on the issuance of U.S. dollar-backed stablecoins for entities under DFS virtual-currency or limited-purpose trust oversight. The letter organizes its expectations around redeemability, reserve assets and attestations. An issuer whose marketing stops at "fully backed" has described only half of what it sells.

What a stablecoin holder is buying is the reserve, the redemption path and the attestation regime. The token is how that reaches them.

What an attestation actually says

An attestation is a time-stamped examination of management's assertions under a defined scope, and it covers much less than a full audit of the risks an issuer faces. The DFS press release accompanying the guidance stressed monthly examination by an independent U.S.-licensed certified public accountant (CPA) applying American Institute of Certified Public Accountants (AICPA) attestation standards. That work typically checks whether the fair value of reserves meets outstanding stablecoin units on the report dates, and whether the reserve conditions set by supervisors are met. It gives no assurance that every banking counterparty will open on Monday.

Circle's public materials describe the same limits from the issuer's side. Its Transparency & Stability page states that a Big Four firm provides monthly third-party assurance that the value of USDC reserves is greater than USDC in circulation, prepared to AICPA standards, alongside weekly reserve disclosure and mint/burn flows. Holders should treat that cadence as part of the product. What the PDF is worth depends on the lag, the scope, whether report dates are sampled at random, and whether liabilities are reconciled as well as assets.

When "backed" still depegs

March 2023 showed that the composition of reserves and access to them matter as much as a monthly letter. After Silicon Valley Bank (SVB) failed, Circle disclosed that about $3.3 billion of USDC reserves, roughly 8 percent of then-total reserves on a book near $40 billion, sat at the failed bank. USDC traded below $1 in secondary markets as redemption pressure rose. A Federal Reserve Board staff FEDS Note reconstructs the sequence: public acknowledgment of trapped uninsured deposits, then solvency concerns measured against Circle's equity, then a surge in primary-market redemptions. After the joint federal announcement that SVB depositors would be made whole, Circle said the $3.3 billion would be available when banks reopened and that, in the company's framing, USDC remained redeemable one-for-one (Circle press room, 13 March 2023).

Monthly attestations came through the episode useful but incomplete. Holders also needed to know, continuously, where the cash sat, which deposits were uninsured and how fast par redemption works when banks are closed. Integrators who had only filed the latest examination PDF were holding a month-old picture of reserves that had moved in a weekend.

Design without redeemable reserves

Algorithmic designs show what a reserve does by failing without one. In May 2022 TerraUSD (UST) lost its dollar peg and collapsed along with LUNA. The Congressional Research Service brief on algorithmic stablecoins and the TerraUSD crash described a dual-token arbitrage mechanism that had none of the cash-and-Treasury reserve stack DFS later codified for supervised USD coins. A Richmond Fed Economic Brief walked through how the algorithmic defense broke under selling pressure. Holders found there was no reliable route to liquid par assets at scale, and that followed directly from how the coin had been designed.

How firms score the reserve path

Treasury teams and payment platforms that hold large stablecoin balances usually look well beyond ticker liquidity.

Feature Useful signal Where it misleads
Reserve mix Cash, T-bills, reverse repo, fund holdings with named custodians “Cash equivalents” that hide uninsured bank concentration
Segregation Assets held for holders, apart from issuer operating cash Legal entity maze with no clear insolvency story
Attestation cadence Monthly AICPA exam + public report lag under 30 days Annual marketing deck with no engagement letter visibility
Redemption rights Who can redeem at par, cutoffs, fees, jurisdiction Retail secondary market liquidity mistaken for primary par claim
Banking day risk Multiple SIIs, known deposit insurance limits, weekend ops plan Assuming “backed” means continuous access through any bank failure

For issuers supervised in New York, DFS guidance in effect scores several of those rows, and Circle's transparency program covers frequency and public PDFs for USDC. Neither removes run dynamics, because money-like liabilities still reward whoever leaves first when confidence drops. If redemption is gated, delayed or limited to large counterparties, smaller holders discover they held a different product from the one the landing page implied.

Issuers compete on disclosure the way money-market funds compete on holdings reports. Weekly reserve pages, named custodians and AICPA examination PDFs earn their keep when redemption desks and market makers actually use them, and they fall short when they say nothing about bank concentration or weekend access. Federal frameworks for payment stablecoins may add reserve and disclosure rules over time, but they would build on what DFS guidance, Circle's transparency program, Terra's failure and the SVB week have already shown about where the product lives.

For payment platforms, treasurers and wallet brands, chain choice and yield wrappers should stay secondary until reserve composition, segregation, attestation scope and contractual redemption are documented from primary sources. Where those fields cannot be filled in, the firm does not know enough about the product to treat the token as cash. Filling them in does not end the work: bank concentration and report lag still need monitoring, because in March 2023 the market priced the reserve path in real time.

Reader Decision Score the reserve path, not the ticker.

Before integrating a payment stablecoin, fill reserve mix, custodian, attestation firm and lag, redemption eligibility, and bank-concentration hypotheses from primary documents.

Market Signal Attestation quality is competing product design.

DFS-style monthly AICPA exams and public reserve pages are becoming table stakes. Markets still reprice when reserve access fails between report dates.
