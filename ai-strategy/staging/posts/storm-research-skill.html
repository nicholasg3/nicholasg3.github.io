Multi-perspective research only works with sources

STORM-style research skill (grounded multi-perspective)

Seed tweet · source: W26 skill S=0.9

The claim

A reusable multi-perspective research skill is worth packaging only if it keeps retrieval and citation. Role-play without sources multiplies fluent error. The viral “Stanford STORM → PhD in minutes” pattern keeps the costume of many expert voices and often drops the spine that made the original system research: external search, source tracking, and synthesis against evidence.

Real STORM work from Stanford’s OVAL lab was retrieval-grounded. Multi-perspective questions were meant to drive search and citation, not to cosplay five personas inside one model’s memory. When the brand stays and the retrieval leaves, the prestige name vouches for the opposite of what the artifact does.

The blind-spot paradox

One model playing Practitioner, Academic, Skeptic, Economist, and Historian does not create five independent investigators. It creates five correlated styles of the same prior. Structural multiplicity is not epistemic independence. The method markets variance reduction and can deliver synchronized mistakes with more confidence.

That is the trap people miss when they only worry about token cost. The deeper failure is verification theater. The fancier the personas, the more the output looks like a panel. A panel drawn from one correlated generator is still one generator. Blind spots are not eliminated. They are costumed.

What skillification does

The useful instinct in the viral thread is packaging: turn a research method into a skill an agent can run. That instinct becomes dangerous when packaging selects for executability over fidelity. Each hop — paper to listicle to prompt pack to SKILL.md — can strip provenance and keep the part that is easiest to automate.

Executable infrastructure outlives fashion. A hollow method that installs cleanly can become default agent behavior long after the thread dies. That is why grounding is not optional decoration. Without it, you are institutionalizing slop with a research-looking filename.

What to build instead

Build the skill as a pipeline, not a costume change:

  • External search with logged queries and retained sources
  • Persona critique that is required to cite those sources or mark claims as unsupported
  • A claim table: statement, source, confidence, disagreement
  • An explicit residual-unknowns section
  • A stop rule when sources conflict or coverage is thin

Skip any of those steps and you are shipping cosplay. Prefer fewer personas with real retrieval over five voices arguing inside a closed context window. Prefer residual uncertainty over a false sense of coverage.

Closing

Multi-perspective research is a good idea. Multi-perspective improvisation is a different product. If you package the method, package the retrieval spine first. Otherwise the Stanford name is not a warrant. It is a costume label on correlated fluency.
