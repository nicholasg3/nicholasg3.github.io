Multi-persona agents often buy fluency, not coverage

Multi-persona “research” blind spot (STORM theater without retrieval)

Seed tweet · source: blog-shortlist #5

A certain kind of post sells research as casting. Give Claude five hats — Practitioner, Academic, Skeptic, Economist, Historian — and you will get “PhD-level” work in minutes. The screenshots look diverse. The voices disagree in tone. The final memo has sections, caveats, and the social proof of simulated expertise. People turn the prompts into SKILL.md files and call it a research stack.

Sometimes that is a useful drafting trick. Often it is marketed as coverage: multi-perspective investigation that eliminates blind spots. That is the claim that needs pressure. One model role-playing a committee can sound diverse while sharing the same blind spots. You bought fluency. You may not have bought investigation.

The claim

Ensemble methods work when errors are decorrelated. Independent readers, different datasets, different incentives, different failure modes. Personas generated by one model, in one context window, with one decoding path, are not that. They are stylistic variance over a correlated prior. The Skeptic and the Historian still drank from the same training distribution. They will often miss the same private fact, the same recent paper, and the same inconvenient primary source.

Real STORM-style systems — Synthesis of Topic Outlines through Retrieval and Multi-perspective Question Asking, from Stanford’s OVAL lab — were built around retrieval and multi-perspective questioning with a citation spine. Viral copies keep the theater and drop the retrieval. The brand then vouches for the opposite of what the artifact does. That is not a small implementation detail. It is the difference between research and cosplay.

Prefer tools that can fail to find a source over tools that always sound complete.

What the theater gets right

Do not throw away the useful part. Multi-perspective prompting can help with:

  • Outline diversity when you are stuck in one framing
  • Checklist generation for risks you already roughly understand
  • Tone experiments for a memo aimed at mixed audiences
  • Surfacing questions you should ask a real expert or a real database

Those are drafting and elicitation benefits. They are real. They are not the same as “the blind spots are gone.” Confusing the two is how teams ship diligence theater into investment memos and strategy decks.

Why it fails as research

Correlated errors. If the model’s prior underweights a region, a regulatory regime, or a class of failure, five personas will underweight it in five accents. Disagreement about adjectives is not disagreement about facts.

No cost to hallucination. A free-form persona can invent a paper, a market size, or a “common industry practice” with the same confidence as a real one. Without retrieval and citation checks, the Skeptic’s job collapses into vibes. The costume of skepticism is cheap; the work of falsification is not.

Token theater. Multi-persona runs are expensive. Cost creates the feeling of thoroughness. People equate spend and structure with epistemic quality. High token burn is not a literature review.

Brand laundering. Attaching “Stanford STORM” or “PhD-level” to a four-prompt paste gives the output institutional glow. Readers inherit the glow. The method’s original load-bearing piece — retrieval-grounded outline synthesis — may never have run.

Skill-file ossification. Once the prompts live in a reusable skill, teams stop inspecting them. The theater becomes infrastructure. New hires treat the skill as a research department. The blind spot becomes a product.

A concrete example of the failure mode

Suppose you ask a multi-persona agent to research “whether we should enter market X with an AI support product.” The Practitioner lists competitor features. The Economist sketches TAM/SAM/SOM. The Skeptic warns about churn. The Historian offers a paragraph about prior waves of automation. The Academic drops three plausible-looking citations.

What you may still not have:

  • A single primary filing, pricing page, or customer interview note
  • Any number tied to your actual conversion funnel
  • Regulatory constraints specific to your entity and geography
  • A falsifiable reason a competitor with the same models would not copy you next quarter

The memo can still feel complete. Completeness of voice is not completeness of evidence. If you cannot click a source and see the claim, you are reading a monologue with props.

What good multi-perspective work looks like

Keep the roles if they help. Force the spine they usually skip.

  1. Retrieval before rhetoric. Each persona’s load-bearing claims need external sources retrieved in-session, not remembered from training.
  2. Citations that can fail. Prefer systems that return “not found” over systems that invent a neat footnote.
  3. Disagreement scored on facts. “The Skeptic was harsher” is useless. “The Skeptic and Practitioner conflict on churn rate; only one has a source” is useful.
  4. Human-owned private facts. Anything the model cannot know — pipeline truth, partner politics, unpublished constraints — is labeled as missing, not filled with plausible filler.
  5. Stop conditions. If retrieval coverage is thin, the run ends in “insufficient sources,” not a confident five-act essay.

That last point is cultural. Organizations hate “insufficient sources” because it looks like the tool failed. It is the tool succeeding. A process that cannot admit ignorance will manufacture certainty for a fee.

How to evaluate a multi-persona skill you already use

Take the last three outputs your team treated as research. For each one, mark every load-bearing claim as:

  • Primary-sourced (link opens, claim matches)
  • Secondary-sourced (credible report, still one step removed)
  • Model-prior (sounds right, no source)
  • Private/unknown (needs human input)

If more than a thin minority of decision-relevant claims are primary-sourced, you do not have a research agent. You have a prose generator with committee cosplay. Keep it for drafting. Do not let it author diligence conclusions without a separate evidence pass.

What to do this week

If you use multi-perspective agents, change the default contract:

  • No persona output enters a decision packet without a retrieval log.
  • Score disagreement on facts and sources, not tone.
  • Budget tokens for search and verification before you budget them for more voices.
  • Rename internal skills honestly: “multi-perspective draft” is fine; “PhD research” is not, unless the citation spine is real.

The market will keep selling hats. Hats are not eyes. Coverage comes from retrieval, independent error, and the willingness to stop when the sources are not there. Fluency is easy to buy. Coverage is what you actually needed.
