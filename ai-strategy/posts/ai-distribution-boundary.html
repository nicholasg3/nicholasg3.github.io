AI Assistants Are Taking Over the Moment a Buyer Decides

AI is changing distribution faster than many business models can adjust. Search is turning into somewhere agents act on a user's behalf, workplace software is starting to execute tasks itself, and browsers are becoming a tool users hand work off to. For operators, the practical question is who controls the point where a buyer's intent turns into an action.

By Nicholas Garcia — Lecturer, NUS School of Computing · Deputy Director, NUS FinTech Lab

For about a decade, digital distribution was messy but familiar. Companies bought ads, fought for search rank, posted on social platforms and built email lists, all in the hope of turning attention into someone who would come back next month.

That arrangement is changing. Google is pushing AI Mode deeper into Search, adding follow-up conversations, personal context, information agents and booking help. OpenAI is extending ChatGPT into cross-app execution, tools, browsers and recurring tasks. Microsoft is selling Copilot and Agent 365 as the enterprise layer for agents that run inside workplace permissions and compliance rules. Read together, these launches move the place where distribution happens.

I call that place the AI distribution ownership line: the point where a buyer's intent gets interpreted, the options get framed, trust gets assigned and one next step becomes the obvious one. On the old web it usually sat on a search result, a review page, a marketplace listing or an email sign-up form. With agents it can sit inside a search answer, a workplace assistant, a browser session or a procurement workflow, and the brand may never see most of what happens there.

An AI surface that can summarize the market, recommend a provider, start a booking or draft the purchase case acts as a strategic gate, and discovery is only one part of what it controls.

Distribution still runs on defaults

Because you can ask it anything in plain language, AI feels open. Language only lowers the cost of asking, though. The system still decides which sources appear, which next steps are one click away, which vendors are integrated, and when an answer seems complete enough that the user stops looking.

AI search therefore matters to companies that have nothing to do with media. When a customer asks for a recommendation or a shortlist, the answer can be compressed before they ever reach the open web. Pages built for search-engine optimization may end up as source material for the model instead of the place the customer lands. A brand can be cited, paraphrased or skipped, or replaced by a completed action such as a booking or a purchase, with no visit to a landing page at all.

Enterprise workflows get the same treatment. When an employee asks a workplace agent for a vendor shortlist, a policy answer or a draft customer plan, the distribution ownership line sits inside the work suite. A vendor that has won over the human buyer still has to get past the assistant, which needs evidence, working integration and a permission model it can operate within.

Where the control points sit

The line is a set of control points spread across several surfaces. Laying them out lets a founder see which one actually decides the outcome before spending scarce effort on content, partnerships, integrations, direct sales or paid reach.

Control point Question Bad strategy
Discovery Where will the buyer's question first be interpreted? Assuming the buyer still begins on the open homepage.
Evidence What machine-readable and human-readable proof makes the company shortlistable? Publishing claims that sound persuasive but cannot be cited, verified, or compared.
Action Which next step can the AI surface complete or tee up? Optimizing awareness while the agent sends action elsewhere.
Context Which personal, team, workflow, or account context changes the recommendation? Treating every visitor as anonymous after the platform has already personalized the decision.
Relationship How does the company earn a reachable relationship outside the gatekeeper? Renting intent forever and calling it growth.
Conversion Where does payment, subscription, booking, trial, or sales handoff actually happen? Letting the easiest checkout own the customer learning loop.
Feedback What signal returns to the company after the agent-mediated interaction? Measuring traffic after the platform has hidden the decision process.

Why AI search raises the stakes on being searchable

Search used to point you somewhere. AI search increasingly does the work itself, acting as clerk, analyst and occasionally builder. Google's 2026 Search updates say so plainly, describing information agents, agentic booking, shopping help and generated experiences. A user may never open a page about a service, because the system has already compared the options, built a tracker or sent them straight to a provider that can finish the job.

Small companies have the harder version of this. Ranking well and writing well still help, but they leave gaps. A company now has to publish its proposition, evidence, prices, constraints and contact details clearly enough that an AI system can describe it without filling holes, since a model faced with missing information will usually invent it.

You can check this without a research budget. Ask whether an agent summarizing your company without visiting the site would get it right enough to create demand. If it would not, the material you have published is too thin.

Why workplace agents change B2B distribution

Selling to businesses has always involved translation inside the buyer's organization. Someone who likes a tool still has to turn it into a business case, a budget request, a security review and a management decision, and AI assistants now do much of that translating. ChatGPT Work pulls context from tools, files, apps and a browser to produce documents and analyses. Microsoft describes Copilot as a place to build guided or autonomous agents with enterprise security and governance, and its partnership with KPMG presents Agent 365 as a way to manage and secure AI agents at scale.

In a lot of enterprise sales, then, the buyer's assistant becomes the first internal analyst to look at a vendor. It compares claims, flags risks, drafts the internal note and points out what evidence is missing. A vendor that impresses a person and then comes up short with the assistant may never get the meeting.

Brand, under these conditions, becomes a practical matter. It is what is left after credible evidence, documents a buyer can use, clean integration, sensible pricing and a promise clear enough to pass through an overloaded buyer's workflow without someone having to reinterpret it at every step.

What a founder should do about it

A founder who tries to be everywhere spreads effort across channels before learning what any one of them does. Choose one primary distribution ownership line and one way to stay in touch with people after they find you, and make both work before adding anything else.

For a strategy publication, one sensible sequence starts with a public archive of durable, linkable essays. A newsletter comes second, once the archive is substantial enough that readers have a reason to return. Payment experiments can wait until that first step works, because a paywall that goes up before the archive has proved itself cuts off readers before any relationship exists.

Other businesses can apply the same approach. A service firm might pair evidence that AI search can find with a consultation call. A developer-tools team might pair documentation with a marketplace integration. A SaaS company might pair procurement-ready pages with security documentation a workplace agent can read. Whichever channel the slide deck names, the company still has to choose its first control point and build for it.

Eight questions to answer before spending more on distribution

Write the answers down before committing another month to distribution work. Answers from a workshop tend to be forgotten by Friday.

  1. Where is a buyer's intent most likely to be interpreted before they reach us?
  2. Which AI or platform surface could compress, distort or redirect that intent?
  3. What evidence has to exist for a human to shortlist us and for an assistant to summarize us accurately?
  4. Which action should be easy from the first serious contact: read, reply, book, subscribe, trial, buy or forward?
  5. How do we stay in touch with someone after the first interaction, on terms we control?
  6. Which conversion path actually works today?
  7. What feedback reaches us when a platform sits between us and the buyer?
  8. Which channel should we ignore on purpose until the first control point works?

The last question matters most for a small company, because AI distribution will keep offering more surfaces than it can handle at once: search answers, agents, browsers, marketplaces, embedded workflows. Founders need to know which one currently decides whether a buyer sees them, trusts them, acts and comes back, and put their effort there first.

Over the next cycle, that means spending on control points before campaigns: evidence an assistant can carry into a buyer's workflow, actions that convert, a way to reach people again, and feedback that comes back to the company. It also means making sure the company is described accurately at decisions it is never invited into, such as an assistant drafting a buyer's purchase case.
