US campus pipelines still shape SEA founder networks

US universities sort and socialize a slice of SEA startup talent. That pipeline moves jobs and capital — and it is not the only talent system that matters.

The claim

US universities remain a major sorting machine for SEA founders and technical talent who later build or invest across the Pacific.

Visa policy, tuition costs, and remote work change the pipeline — but alumni networks still move capital and hiring.

How to ground it

Use university and immigration statistics for volume claims. Use founder surveys only as secondary color.

National Science Foundation and SEVIS-related public stats help bound international student flows.

Operator consequences

Companies that ignore alumni networks miss warm distribution. Companies that only hire that network miss local operators without US credentials.

What to do

Map your hiring and angel sources by education geography. Rebalance if you are accidentally building a single-pipeline monoculture.

For SEA go-to-market, local operators often out-execute imported prestige.
