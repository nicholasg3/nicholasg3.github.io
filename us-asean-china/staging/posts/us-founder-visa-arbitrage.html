Visa and tax regimes are competitive strategy

Founder location between Singapore, the US, and China is often discussed as lifestyle. It is also product strategy: who you can hire, bank, fundraise from, and sell to without friction. Treat mobility rules as infrastructure.

The claim

A founder's passport, visa, and tax residence change banking access, equity plan design, travel cadence, and which customers trust you onshore.

Teams that treat this as an ops afterthought rediscover it during fundraising, immigration renewals, or enterprise security reviews.

Triangles have costs

US–Singapore–China mobility can unlock markets and capital. It also creates permanent substance, PE, and compliance questions. Clever structures that look clean on Twitter fail in diligence.

Primary immigration and tax authority pages beat influencer relocation threads.

What companies actually optimize

Hiring pools, founder presence requirements for investors, data residency, and ability to visit customers without visa roulette.

Remote-first is not regime-optional. Someone still holds the bank account and the employment contracts.

What to do

Write a one-page mobility map: where HQ is, where people work, where IP sits, where revenue is booked, and which visas are load-bearing.

Revisit the map before each fundraise and each new country hire plan.
