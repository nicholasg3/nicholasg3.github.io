Founder visas are strategy

Pass thresholds in Singapore and entrepreneur parole rules in the United States allocate where companies incorporate, hire, and raise.

SINGAPORE — Founders still talk about “where the talent is” as if geography were weather. Immigration instruments treat geography as policy. Salary floors, investment thresholds, and discretionary parole rules decide who can sit in a company director seat, open a bank account, or stay long enough to ship a product. Between Singapore and the United States—two poles of the US–ASEAN–China talent triangle—those instruments are competitive strategy.

China remains a third pole for manufacturing, research, and domestic market access, with its own work-permit and talent-scheme stack. This memo focuses on the two systems with the clearest public founder-facing instruments for cross-border operators: Singapore’s work-pass suite and the U.S. International Entrepreneur Rule. The point is not lifestyle ranking. The point is that incorporation and hiring plans respond to published thresholds.

Visa and talent regimes are competitive strategy for multi-country founders—not HR admin after the deck is done.

Singapore’s founder and talent passes

Singapore’s Ministry of Manpower publishes the EntrePass for foreign entrepreneurs who start and operate a business in Singapore that is venture-backed or possesses innovative technologies. The pass sits beside the broader work-pass system. MOM’s work passes overview describes the Employment Pass for foreign professionals, managers, executives, and technicians, with a qualifying salary of at least S$5,600 a month and the Complementarity Assessment Framework (COMPASS) for candidates. Those numbers move over time; the structure does not. Salary floors and points frameworks are industrial policy for human capital.

Enterprise Singapore’s Startup SG ecosystem points founders to EntrePass as part of the startup support surface. Eligibility criteria published by MOM and explained across official Startup SG materials typically require a private limited company path, meaningful founder ownership, and innovation or funding signals (for example venture funding from recognised sources, accelerator acceptance, IP, or prior entrepreneurial track record—exact combinations follow current MOM eligibility pages). The design selects for venture-shaped firms, not every sole trader.

At the high end, the Economic Development Board’s Tech.Pass targets established tech leaders with salary and experience thresholds—including a last-drawn fixed monthly salary of at least S$22,500 and multi-year leadership in large or well-funded tech firms or major tech VCs, per EDB’s published criteria. That is not a mass founder visa. It is a signal about which talent tier Singapore wants to make friction-light.

The U.S. International Entrepreneur Rule

The United States does not offer a simple, dedicated “startup visa” with the clarity founders want. It does offer, under the Department of Homeland Security’s International Entrepreneur Rule, a discretionary grant of parole—authorized stay—for entrepreneurs whose U.S. startup is shown to provide a significant public benefit. USCIS is explicit: this is parole, not a visa classification, and work authorization is tied to the startup.

Thresholds adjust with inflation. For applications filed on or after 1 October 2024, USCIS states that startups must generally show at least $311,071 in qualified investment from qualifying investors, or at least $124,429 in qualified government awards or grants, or partial funding plus alternative compelling evidence of rapid growth potential. Initial parole may run up to 2.5 years, with possible re-parole for up to another 2.5 years (maximum five). Ownership and active role tests apply (including substantial ownership, treated as at least 10 percent at initial adjudication under the rule’s guidance). Spouses may seek employment authorization after parole; the entrepreneur works for the startup entity.

Those numbers are strategy inputs. A founder choosing between incorporating a U.S. C-corp for fundraising optics and building first in Singapore is partly choosing between venture-network density and pass predictability. Parole is discretionary and temporary. EntrePass and Employment Pass are structured Singapore instruments with their own renewal and eligibility tests. Neither path is free. Both are legible enough that sophisticated counsel prices them into term sheets and hiring plans.

What multi-country teams actually optimize

Instrument Who it selects Strategic implication
Singapore EntrePass Innovative / venture-shaped foreign founders HQ and early ops in a predictable city-state system
Singapore Employment Pass PMETs meeting salary + COMPASS Hire senior staff under salary floors, not informal remote fiction
Singapore Tech.Pass Top-tier tech operators/founders Compete for scarce executive talent with a dedicated pass
U.S. IER parole U.S. startups with qualified capital or grants Temporary presence near U.S. capital markets; not permanent status

Teams that treat mobility as strategy keep a simple scoreboard: which executives can lawfully work where, which bank accounts can open under which directors, which investors require which founder location for diligence, and which renewal cliffs force product freezes. English Twitter debates about “digital nomad visas” rarely touch those rows. Board packages should.

China’s instruments remain relevant for operators with factories, research labs, or China-market GTM, but they are not a single founder-pass analogue to EntrePass or IER. Multi-country companies often end up with a portfolio: Singapore for regional HQ and banking, U.S. entities for capital, and separate China work authorization for on-the-ground staff. Portfolio design is the strategy. Pretending immigration is admin is how companies discover, late, that the only person who can sign is on the wrong side of a border.

Optionality has a cost structure. Maintaining dual entities means dual compliance, dual banking KYC, and dual tax advice. Missing a renewal cliff can freeze hiring in the market that was supposed to be the “easy” hub. U.S. parole under IER is temporary and discretionary; it is a bridge toward other statuses, not a permanent founder visa by another name. Singapore passes are clearer instruments, but they still demand ongoing business substance, salary compliance for EP holders, and renewal evidence for EntrePass holders. Strategy is choosing which frictions the company can pay for on purpose.

Founder mobility between Singapore and the United States is priced in public: salary floors, COMPASS, EntrePass innovation tests, Tech.Pass thresholds, and IER investment numbers. Those figures move with policy and inflation. The competitive fact does not. Jurisdictions that want startups publish rails. Founders who treat those rails as strategy get further than founders who treat them as afterthoughts.
