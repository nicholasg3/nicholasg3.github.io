English founder media is not the customer

In large Southeast Asian markets, English-language founder coverage can create a parallel public that impresses capital while the paying user, the support ticket, and the regulator still operate in another language.

A company can win the English internet and still fail the market that pays. That is not a vibe complaint about “authenticity.” It is a measurement error: founder media, demo days, and cross-border podcasts sample one public; revenue samples another.

The particular claim is narrow. In non-English-majority markets — Indonesia is the clearest large case — English-language founder discourse is a biased instrument for demand. Boards that treat English narrative heat as product-market fit will mis-hire, mis-localize, and mis-forecast.

Two publics, one deck

Every regional startup lives in at least two publics. One is the capital and media public: investors, English-language tech press, conference circuits, LinkedIn threads. The other is the customer public: the person who completes checkout, calls support, or complains to a local regulator.

Those publics do not have to share a language. When they do not, English coverage can rise while local conversion stalls. The deck still looks global. The cohort charts do not.

Regional English outlets exist for a reason. Tech in Asia, for example, runs as English-language Asia tech media aimed at a regional and international audience. That is a real market for attention and fundraising. It is not a census of who buys fried chicken on an app in Surabaya.

Indonesia is digital at scale without being English-native

Use Indonesia as the stress case, not as a stand-in for all of ASEAN. Singapore’s language ecology is different. The Philippines’ is different. Indonesia is where the parallel-public problem is hardest to hand-wave.

Indonesia’s constitution states that the national language is Indonesian (Bahasa Indonesia). That is not folklore. It is Article 36 of the 1945 Constitution as amended, as published in the Constitute Project text. Product copy, consumer protection notices, and government interfaces sit inside that national-language reality even when a founder’s Series A narrative does not.

Scale is not the same as English. DataReportal’s Digital 2024: Indonesia report (We Are Social / Meltwater series) put Indonesia at 185.3 million internet users in early 2024, with penetration at 66.5 percent of a population of about 278.7 million. Those figures establish a large digital addressable base. They do not establish that base’s working language for commerce.

If your growth model assumes the English-speaking fraction of that 185 million behaves like the whole, you have smuggled a language assumption into a TAM slide.

English proficiency is a weak proxy — and the proxy is not flattering

There is no perfect public census of “can complete this English checkout.” Private test vendors publish relative scores. Treat them as rough instruments, then still look at them.

The EF English Proficiency Index page for Indonesia (retrieved 2026-07-31) places Indonesia at global rank #80 with an EF EPI score of 471, below the global average score of 488 shown on the same page. EF’s own materials band scores into proficiency categories; the point for operators is comparative, not mystical: Indonesia is not clustered with high-proficiency small open economies on this instrument.

Two cautions, stated plainly. First, EF measures people who take EF’s tests, not a random household survey. Second, “low relative English score” is not “cannot learn product English.” It is still enough to reject the lazy inference that English founder Twitter is a demand sensor for Indonesia.

Theory: lingua franca for elites is not domestic majority speech

Applied linguistics has a clean way to hold two true statements at once. English can function as a lingua franca among multilingual professionals across ASEAN — useful in regional meetings, contracts, and investor updates — without becoming the language of domestic mass publics.

Andy Kirkpatrick’s English as a Lingua Franca in ASEAN is the standard book-length statement of that multilingual model. Use it as a theory hook: ELF explains why your board meeting can be in English while your five-star app reviews are not.

The failure mode for founders is collapsing ELF into “the market speaks English.” ELF is a bridge among already-mobile actors. Customer acquisition is often a different bridge.

What breaks in the company when you confuse the publics

Hiring optimizes for English storytelling: growth leads who write crisp investor updates and weak local-language community ops.

Support tickets arrive in Bahasa (or mixed code) while the help center and the LLM fine-tune stay English-first. Resolution times blow up; App Store ratings tell the truth the deck did not.

Compliance and consumer notices need national-language precision. English legalese pasted into a settings screen is not “global UX.” It is a regulatory and trust defect.

Paid growth tests English creative because the performance team lives in English tools. Local competitors run the same funnel in the language of the feed and win on CAC.

What to measure instead of podcast mentions

For any SEA market entry review, require a language scoreboard next to the funnel:

Signal What it measures What it does not measure
English founder press / podcast hits Capital and media public attention Willingness to pay among local users
% of support tickets by language Actual service language load Whether your roadmap funds that load
Checkout completion by UI language Friction in the money path Brand “premium” stories
Share of UGC / reviews by language Where social proof is produced Investor narrative quality
Regulator / bank partner language of record Compliance surface Demo-day applause

If English narrative metrics move and these do not, you are succeeding at the parallel public.

What this is not claiming

This is not an argument against English. English remains the practical language of much cross-border capital, documentation, and multi-country teams. Kirkpatrick’s ELF frame says that can be true without rewriting domestic demand.

This is not “never publish in English.” Publish in English for the capital public. Instrument the customer public separately. The bug is substituting one for the other in a board pack.

This is not uniform across ASEAN. Run the same language scoreboard in Vietnam, Thailand, and the Philippines and you will get different splits. The method travels. The Indonesia numbers do not paste onto Singapore.

Closing

English founder media is a market. It is a market for attention, status, and capital allocation. Treat it as such. Do not confuse it with the market for your SKU.

If you need one operating rule: no growth review without the language of revenue. Until that table exists, English heat is entertainment.
