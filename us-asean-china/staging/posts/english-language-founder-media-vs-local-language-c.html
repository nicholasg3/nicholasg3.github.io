English founder media is not the customer

English-language founder coverage, demo days, and cross-border podcasts sample a capital public. Checkout, support, and regulators sample another. Treat the gap as a market-entry test.

Founder media in Southeast Asia often runs in English. So do many pitch decks, conference stages, and investor updates. That English layer is a real market for attention and capital. It is a weak instrument for demand in large national markets where customers buy, complain, and read terms in another language. The Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) label makes the region look unified; language of commerce still splits country by country.

The operating mistake is simple. Teams watch English narrative heat and call it product-market fit. Boards then fund hires, creative, and localization in the wrong order. The company optimizes for the public that writes checks to funds while the public that pays for the product stays under-instrumented.

Score the language of revenue the way you score a first-country wedge: name the buyer public, the reuse path, and the false positive.

Two publics on one deck

A regional startup serves at least two publics. The capital public includes investors, English-language tech press, conference circuits, and LinkedIn threads. The customer public includes the person who completes checkout, opens a support ticket, or files a complaint with a local authority.

Those publics can share a language. In many Southeast Asian markets they do not. When they diverge, English coverage can rise while local conversion stays flat. The deck still looks global. The cohort chart does not.

English regional outlets exist because capital and talent move across borders. Tech in Asia is one clear example of English-language Asia tech media built for a regional and international audience. That audience matters for fundraising and hiring. It does not census who buys on an app in a secondary Indonesian city.

Indonesia as the stress case

Run the test on Indonesia first when the product aims at mass or mid-market Southeast Asian demand. Singapore, the Philippines, and Vietnam each have different language ecologies. Indonesia is where English founder narrative and domestic commercial language pull farthest apart.

Indonesia’s constitution names Indonesian (Bahasa Indonesia) as the national language. Article 36 of the 1945 Constitution as amended, in the Constitute Project text, puts that in law. Consumer notices, many government interfaces, and a large share of mass-market product copy sit inside that national-language reality even when a Series A narrative does not.

Digital scale is real and still leaves the language question open. DataReportal’s Digital 2024: Indonesia report put the country at 185.3 million internet users in early 2024, with penetration at 66.5 percent of a population of about 278.7 million. Those figures establish a large digital base. A total-addressable-market slide that treats the English-speaking fraction as the whole of that base has already failed the language-of-revenue test.

English skill as a rough instrument

Public household censuses rarely answer “can this user finish an English checkout.” Private test vendors publish relative scores. Use them as comparative instruments with a clear sample definition.

The EF English Proficiency Index page for Indonesia (page snapshot used for this draft) places Indonesia at global rank #80 with an EF EPI score of 471, against a global average of 488 on the same page. EF Education First is a private testing firm; the sample is people who take EF’s tests. On this instrument Indonesia sits below the global average, far from the high-proficiency cluster of small open economies. English founder Twitter is a poor demand sensor for Indonesian mass market on that evidence.

Lingua franca for mobile actors

English still does real work in the region. Multi-country teams, contracts, and investor updates often run in English. Applied linguistics already has a clean frame for that: English as a lingua franca among multilingual professionals across ASEAN.

Andy Kirkpatrick’s English as a Lingua Franca in ASEAN is the book-length statement of that multilingual model. A lingua-franca frame explains why a board meeting can be English while five-star app reviews and support threads stay local. Founders who treat lingua-franca traffic as proof of domestic majority demand misread the instrument.

The language-of-revenue test

Borrow the structure from the ASEAN market-entry and stablecoin distribution tests. A language signal is useful when it changes a commercial fact. It is a false positive when it only flatters the deck.

Test Useful signal False positive
Buyer public The people who pay use the same language as your primary product surface and support path. English press and podcasts rise while checkout and renewals stay local-language and flat.
Support load Ticket language matches staffing and help-center coverage. English-first help center; local-language tickets pile up and ratings fall.
Checkout friction Completion rates by UI language show where money actually clears. English creative wins awards; local UI still owns conversion.
Social proof Reviews and user-generated content in the revenue language produce the next cohort. English Twitter praise with empty local review surfaces.
Compliance surface Terms, disclosures, and partner contracts exist in the language of record for the market. English legalese pasted into settings while the regulator and bank partner work in the national language.

Put that table in the growth review next to funnel metrics. When English narrative moves and these five stay still, the company is succeeding at the capital public.

What breaks inside the company

Hiring tilts toward English storytelling. Growth leads write crisp investor updates. Community and support in the revenue language stay thin.

Paid acquisition tests English creative because the performance team lives in English tools and English dashboards. Local competitors run the same funnel in the language of the feed and win on customer acquisition cost.

Product ships English-first microcopy. Localization arrives as a late sprint after churn already told the story.

Compliance notices stay in English because legal counsel drafts in English. The trust failure shows up as support load and partner friction, then as a regulatory question.

How to run the pilot

Keep the experiment narrow, reversible, and explicit — the same discipline as a stablecoin or first-country pilot:

  1. Name the revenue language for the wedge market (one primary, not “ASEAN multilingual”).
  2. Name the capital language for fundraising and multi-country team work.
  3. Instrument checkout completion, support tickets, and reviews by language for 30 days.
  4. Fund headcount and creative against the revenue-language gaps first when those series diverge from English narrative metrics.
  5. Write a stop rule: if English heat keeps rising while revenue-language conversion does not, cut English-only growth spend until the scoreboard catches up.

Publish in English for the capital public when that public is the job. Instrument the customer public on its own scoreboard. The bug is one board pack that treats the first public as proof of the second.

Closing

English founder media is a market for attention, status, and capital allocation. Run it as that market. Score demand in the language where money clears.

Operating rule: no growth review without the language-of-revenue table. Until that table exists, English heat is entertainment with a fundraising side effect.
