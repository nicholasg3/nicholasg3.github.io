Digital trade texts are not your operating system

ASEAN digital economy agreements and bilateral digital trade chapters set aspirations and some rules. Day-to-day market access still runs through national regulators, data rules, and payment licenses. Do not confuse treaty language with go-to-market clearance.

The claim

Policy decks celebrate digital economy frameworks and DEFA-style negotiations as if they create a single digital market. They do not automatically harmonize licensing, content rules, or data localization.

Treat agreements as directional constraints and lobbying surfaces. Treat national gatekeepers as the shipping checklist.

What agreements can and cannot do

They can reduce some customs friction for digital products, set cooperation language, and signal investment intent.

They rarely replace a payments license, a content takedown order, or a sector regulator's cloud preference.

Founder misuse pattern

Citing a regional agreement in a pitch as proof of ASEAN-wide launch readiness. Sophisticated counterparties will ask for entity-level permissions anyway.

Better: show country sequence, local counsel memos, and data maps.

What to do

Keep a two-layer policy tracker: (1) regional texts you monitor, (2) national obligations you implement.

Only promote regional agreement claims that change a concrete product constraint you currently face.
