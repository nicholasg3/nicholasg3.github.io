## Facts that must appear in the essay (with support)

1. **Singapore container scale remains first in SEA.** MPA-based reporting: ~**44.7 million TEUs** in 2025 (+8.6% y/y); second globally behind Shanghai in World Shipping Council-style rankings as reported.  
   Supports: Business Times, 13 Jan 2026.

2. **Port Klang hit top-10 status on 2024 volumes.** MOT Malaysia: **14.64 million TEUs** in 2024 (+4.1% from 14.06m in 2023); Lloyd’s List ranking path 13th (2022) → 11th (2023) → **10th (2024/2025 list)**.  
   Supports: MOT media release PDF.

3. **Port of Tanjung Pelepas is a peer, not a footnote.** MOT: PTP **12.25 million TEUs** in 2024 (+16.9% from 10.48m in 2023), ~15th globally both years. Malaysia as country fifth-largest by container throughput in 2024 (MOT claim).  
   Supports: MOT media release.

4. **Relative growth can favor peers even when Singapore’s absolute lead is large.** Analyst quote in BT: Singapore’s 2025 box growth below PTP’s 14.5% and above Port Klang’s 3.4%.  
   Supports: Business Times citing Tan Hua Joo / Linerlytica.

5. **Services stack matters.** Singapore records high bunker sales, ship registry gains, and international shipping group presence — competition is not TEUs alone.  
   Supports: Business Times 2025 maritime wrap.

## Theory hook

- **Economic geography / entrepôt competition:** hubs compete for relay cargo and for high-value maritime services.
- **Network effects:** schedule density attracts more schedules; capacity investments try to break or reinforce that loop.
