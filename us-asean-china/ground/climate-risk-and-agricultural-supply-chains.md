# Ground brief — climate-risk-and-agricultural-supply-chains

**Status:** grounded (2026-07-31)  
**Vertical:** us-asean-china

**Claim (particular):** Physical climate risk in Southeast Asia’s agricultural cores—especially drought and salinity in the Mekong Delta rice system—is **operational and trade risk**, not only a long-horizon ESG footnote: yield, export availability, and finance exposure move together when river, rain, and sea-level signals deteriorate.

## Facts that must appear in the essay (with support)

1. **Mekong Delta is a rice-export engine.** Multiple research and official accounts state the Delta supplies on the order of **~90% of Vietnam’s rice exports** and a large share of fruit and seafood output.  
   Supports: peer-reviewed Mekong rice studies; Vietnamese MAE English summaries.

2. **MRC frames the Lower Mekong as climate-vulnerable.** Rising temperatures, changing rainfall intensity, floods, droughts, and sea-level-related salinity are already damaging crops, homes, and fisheries.  
   Supports: https://www.mrcmekong.org/climate-change/

3. **Drought and salinity are linked operational shocks, not abstract “climate.”** Saltwater intrusion into low-lying paddy during dry seasons reduces yields and forces planting decisions.  
   Supports: open-access Mekong drought/salinity literature; MRC narrative.

4. **Climate risk transmits into supply-chain and trade-finance clocks.** When exportable volumes tighten, buyers, warehouse finance, and insurance pricing face higher physical and basis risk.  
   Supports: general commodity risk logic + documented export concentration — do not invent a named bank loss.

## Theory hook

- **Physical climate risk as operational risk** in agricultural GVCs.
- **Geographic concentration risk:** export dependence on a climate-exposed delta is a single-point failure for global rice buyers.
