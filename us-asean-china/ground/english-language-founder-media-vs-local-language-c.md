# Ground brief — english-language-founder-media-vs-local-language-c

**Status:** grounded (2026-07-31)  
**Vertical:** us-asean-china  
**Claim (particular):** English-language founder media and pitch culture in SEA can create a **parallel public** that looks like market traction to investors while the paying customer, support load, and regulator still operate in national/local languages — especially in large non-English-majority markets such as Indonesia.

## Facts that must appear in the essay (with support)

1. **Indonesia’s national language is Indonesian (Bahasa Indonesia).**  
   Supports: Constitute Project constitution text, Article 36.

2. **Indonesia is a large digital market in absolute users, not an English-native market.**  
   Supports: DataReportal Digital 2024 Indonesia — 185.3 million internet users; 66.5% penetration; population ~278.7 million (early 2024).

3. **Measured English proficiency for Indonesia is not “high” on EF’s index.**  
   Supports: EF EPI Indonesia page — global rank **#80**, score **471** (site snapshot retrieved 2026-07-31; EF labels proficiency bands on the same page family). Note: EF is a private test vendor, not a census — use as **proxy for relative English skill among test-takers**, not as “% of population speaks English.”

4. **English in ASEAN is widely theorized as a lingua franca among multilinguals, not a replacement for local publics.**  
   Supports: Kirkpatrick, *English as a Lingua Franca in ASEAN* (Routledge). Use as theory hook, not as a statistic.

5. **Founder-facing tech media that matter for fundraising often publish in English.**  
   Supports: Tech in Asia about/site as example of English-language regional startup media. This supports “parallel public,” not “all customers read Tech in Asia.”

## Theory hook

- **Publics / dual audiences** (Fraser-style multiple publics, used lightly): capital and media publics vs customer publics.
- **English as lingua franca (ELF)** in ASEAN (Kirkpatrick): English connects elites across borders without implying domestic majority proficiency.
- **Market orientation:** language of revenue vs language of narrative.

## What this brief does **not** establish

- Exact % of Indonesian consumers who can complete an English app checkout.
- That every SEA country matches Indonesia (Singapore and Philippines differ sharply).
- That English media is “bad” — only that it is a **biased sample of demand**.
