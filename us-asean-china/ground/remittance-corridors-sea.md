# Ground brief — remittance-corridors-sea

**Status:** grounded (2026-07-31)

**Claim (particular):** In Southeast Asia, **family remittance corridors remain the demand that legitimizes mass-market payment products**. Corridor volume, price (as a share of principal), and last-mile cash-out still set more of the product bar than English fintech branding.

## Facts that must appear in the essay (with support)

1. **Official remittance flows to LMICs were about $656 billion in 2023.**  
   Supports: World Bank remittances brief / Brief 40 framing (also World Bank remittances topic page).

2. **The Philippines was among the top global recipients (~$39 billion in 2023 per Brief 40).**  
   Supports: Migration and Development Brief 40 summary in World Bank documents and Migration Data Portal.

3. **Global average cost of sending ~USD 200 remains ~6%+ (roughly double the SDG 3% target); banks are typically the expensive channel; digital/mobile cheaper on average.**  
   Supports: Migration Data Portal synthesis of Brief 40 (Q4 2023 ~6.4% global average; East Asia & Pacific and South Asia ~5.8%; banks ~12%; mobile operators ~4.4% average cost in that framing). RPW site states global average cost monitoring (site figure ~6.36% on retrieval framing).

4. **SEA fintech legitimacy often rides on existing migration corridors, not only card-style consumer fintech.**  
   Supports: BSP digital payments report (remittances as a use case in measurement); GCash/OFW remittance product expansion reporting as real operator response — use as product evidence, not market-share claim without primary.

## Theory hook

- **Migration–development nexus:** remittances as household finance and current-account support.
- **Corridor economics:** price, speed, cash-out density, and AML/KYC frictions are product parameters by corridor pair, not “ASEAN average.”
- **Payment systems as inclusion infrastructure:** last-mile cash-out and receive accounts matter as much as send-side UX.
