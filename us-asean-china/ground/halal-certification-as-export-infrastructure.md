# Ground brief — halal-certification-as-export-infrastructure

**Status:** grounded (2026-07-31)  
**Vertical:** us-asean-china  
**Rank:** 24  
**Claim (particular):** Halal certification functions as **export infrastructure** for Muslim-majority markets: it is a multi-authority system of audits, labels, foreign-body recognition lists, and phased mandatory rules that can block or delay shelf access even when the physical product is already manufactured.

## Facts that must appear in the essay (with support)

1. **Indonesia’s mandatory frame is statutory.** Law No. 33 of 2014 on Halal Product Assurance creates the BPJPH-centred system; certificates recognize product “halalness” based on MUI fatwa pathway as described in the law text.  
   Supports: FAOLEX / official Law 33/2014 PDF.

2. **Enforcement for broad F&B was extended, not abandoned.** U.S. ITA reports GR No. 42/2024 granted a two-year extension until no later than 17 October 2026 for importers and retailers to comply fully with F&B halal requirements (meat products treated more strictly; positive-list exemptions for some minimally processed goods).  
   Supports: trade.gov “Indonesia Food and Beverage Halal Certification Extended” (updated 4 Feb 2025).

3. **Malaysia runs a foreign-body recognition system, not open-door acceptance of any overseas logo.** JAKIM appoints and lists Recognised Foreign Halal Certification Bodies; products and certifiers must match the list and scopes.  
   Supports: JAKIM FHCB procedures / myehalal recognition pages.

4. **Exporters can hold a valid home-market cert and still fail destination recognition.** Imported products may need BPJPH-pathway recognition or an MRA-linked body; origin label alone is not enough when mutual recognition is incomplete.  
   Supports: trade.gov on foreign body accreditation; secondary confirmations of MRA pathway.

5. **Survey signal of incomplete retail readiness.** Higher-end supermarket survey cited by U.S. ITA: up to ~25% of products still not meeting Indonesia’s certification and labeling requirements at the time of the extension debate.  
   Supports: trade.gov note (attribute as survey reported by ITA, not independent census).

## Theory hook

- **Standards as market access / non-tariff measures:** certification intermediaries convert religious compliance into tradeable product status.
- **Institutional complementarity:** national authorities (BPJPH, JAKIM) plus private/foreign certifiers form a layered gate, not a single global logo.
