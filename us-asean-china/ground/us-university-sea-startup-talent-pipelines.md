# Ground brief — us-university-sea-startup-talent-pipelines

**Status:** grounded (2026-07-31)  
**Vertical:** us-asean-china  
**Claim (particular):** US universities and post-study work rules form an **institutional pipeline** that sorts and socializes a measurable slice of Southeast Asian founder and technical talent; that pipeline is policy-built, not accidental, and it coexists with local talent systems that do not run through US campuses.

## Facts that must appear in the essay (with support)

1. **US international enrollment is large and STEM-heavy.**  
   Supports: IIE Open Doors 2025 — 1,177,766 international students in 2024/25; 57% in STEM fields; OPT reached 294,253 (+21%).

2. **Vietnam is the largest ASEAN sender to US campuses among major SEA sources.**  
   Supports: Open Doors 2023/24 via US Embassy Hanoi — 22,066 Vietnamese students; Embassy states Vietnam is largest ASEAN source. Secondary summaries: Indonesia ~8,348; Thailand ~5,310; Philippines ~4,100 (2023/24).

3. **The pipeline is institutional (OPT, SEVIS, university sorting), not only individual ambition.**  
   Supports: Open Doors OPT series; DHS Study in the States framing of SEVIS-certified institutions; NSF framing of foreign-born S&E workforce.

4. **Real founder case with US degrees exists and is public.**  
   Supports: Nadiem Makarim — Brown University undergrad, Harvard Business School MBA; founded Gojek; later Indonesian Minister of Education. Use biographical primaries (company/government bios), not invented composites.

5. **Local systems still matter; US pipeline is not the only talent market.**  
   Supports: NSF note on East/Southeast Asia S&E capacity growth; do not claim US degrees dominate all SEA founder formation.

## Theory hook

- **Human capital / diaspora networks:** mobility creates both skills and soft capital markets.
- **Institutional sorting:** universities + visas as selection machines (not pure merit markets).
- **Dual embeddedness:** returnees bridge US capital norms and home-market operations.
