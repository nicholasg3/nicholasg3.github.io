# Ground brief — cross-border-e-commerce-returns-logistics

**Status:** grounded (2026-07-31)

**Claim (particular):** In cross-border e-commerce into and across Southeast Asia, **reverse logistics and customs treatment dominate unit economics** more than creative conversion: a returned parcel re-enters duty, tax, and multi-hop last-mile cost structures that outbound marketing never prices.

## Facts that must appear in the essay (with support)

1. **Cross-border logistics cost structure is not domestic logistics with a border.** ERIA volume states international e-commerce faces nearly all issues of other trade forms and is costlier/more complex than single-country distribution.  
   Supports: ERIA *E-commerce Connectivity in ASEAN*.

2. **De minimis rules reprice small parcels.** ESCAP analysis: de minimis applies to traditional and e-commerce shipments; Asia-Pacific thresholds often below $100 in many countries — low thresholds pull more parcels into full duty/tax processes.  
   Supports: UNESCAP STII No.91.

3. **ASEAN is not one customs surface.** Different national de minimis and documentation rules mean a multi-country return policy is a compliance product, not a UX toggle.  
   Supports: World Bank / ESCAP comparative framing.

4. **Forward-path innovations (bonded warehouses) do not automatically solve reverse path.** Regional fulfilment hubs cut outbound latency; returns still need seller-side disposition, possible re-export, or local secondary sales under rules.  
   Supports: logistics logic + ERIA complexity claim; avoid inventing Lazada return %.
