# Ground brief — chinese-diaspora-parallel-economies

**Status:** grounded (2026-07-31)  
**Vertical:** us-asean-china  
**Claim (particular):** Overseas Chinese commercial networks in Southeast Asia form durable parallel rails — family control, dialect trust, cross-border capital — that can lower transaction costs for some entrants and raise political risk when host politics frames them as a separate economy.

## Facts for essay

1. **Named analytic frame: “bamboo network” popularized by Weidenbaum & Hughes (1996).** Supports: strategy-business 1998 essay summarizing book; Wikipedia for term currency only as secondary.
2. **Documented examples in Weidenbaum (1998): Salim Group (Liem Sioe Liong) in Indonesia; Charoen Pokphand (CP) in Thailand; Li Ka-shing structures in Hong Kong; Kuok Group Malaysia; Bangkok Bank / Sophonpanich.** Supports: strategy-business article with specifics (e.g., Salim ~5% of Indonesian economy claim as author estimate; treat as author’s claim, not official GDP audit).
3. **Operating pattern: family control, trust-based finance, multi-country interlocking holdings, preference for intermediate sectors (trade, real estate, finance) over branded consumer goods.** Supports: same.
4. **Parallel rails are not “illegal by definition” — many are listed conglomerates — but they are not the same as a uniform national SME market.** Supports: dual-structure claim.
