# Ground brief — singapore-ceiling-coinbase-policy-scorecard

**Status:** grounded (2026-08-03)
**Vertical:** us-asean-china
**Context:** publishes before Nick's 6 Aug 2026 NUS FinTech Lab podcast with Hassan Ahmed (Coinbase, Country Director Singapore); analytical, not adversarial.
**Claim (particular):** Hassan Ahmed's April 2025 Coinbase blog post asked Singapore for seven specific policy moves to become a "global crypto leader." Fifteen months later, MAS has delivered on the wholesale/institutional asks (BLOOM, Project Guardian/GL1 expansion, a stablecoin framework, an April 2026 prudential consultation) and held the line on every retail ask (marketing guardrails, retail access, no strategic Bitcoin reserve). Read through Edward Kane's regulatory-dialectic frame, that split looks less like foot-dragging and more like a considered choice: MAS is building wholesale rails and letting Hong Kong and the UAE compete for retail-facing crypto volume.

## Theory hook

- **Kane's regulatory dialectic** (load-bearing): MAS's split response — grant wholesale, hold retail — reads as an adaptive move in an ongoing back-and-forth with industry, not paralysis. Kane's model predicts regulators respond selectively to where they see systemic and reputational risk building, not uniformly to every industry ask.
- **Stigler/Peltzman capture theory** (counter-frame, explicitly argued against): if MAS were captured by the industry actors lobbying it (Coinbase among them), the retail ask — the one most directly requested and most publicly argued for — should have moved. It did not. The wholesale asks that moved are also the ones that keep control inside regulated banks and MAS-vetted institutions, the opposite of what capture-by-crypto-firms would predict.
- **Zetzsche et al. on sandboxes**: names the specific instrument Ahmed asked for and that MAS has not built a crypto-specific version of, even while running a general fintech sandbox since 2016.

## Real case

The Hong Kong/UAE contrast: both jurisdictions Ahmed named as competitive threats in April 2025 have since moved further on the *retail-adjacent* products (HKMA stablecoin licences aimed at HKD-referenced consumer-facing tokens; UAE's unified register lowering entry friction for exchanges) than Singapore has. MAS moved further on wholesale/institutional plumbing (BLOOM, GL1 industry-association expansion) than either.
